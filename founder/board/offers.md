# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Stage scores: Stage 1 (alloys) 3/10. Stage 2 (used cars) 5/10. Stage 3 (prestige hire) 3/10, and mostly undefined. Overall 4/10: Stage 2 is the only stage with an offer worth building on, and the ladder only works if Stage 1 and Stage 3 each earn their place.

## What is strong
- Stage 2 has a real stack forming: history check, fresh MOT and inspection report sold with a £2,000-£5,000 car. That removes the buyer's biggest fear (buying a lemon from a stranger), which is the "likelihood" part of the value equation. This is the only part of the plan that is not a price play.
- Stage 2 buyers are named and split (first-car/parents 35%, commuters 40%, families 25%). The pain is real: they need a car, have money (it is a £2k-£5k purchase), and are easy to reach online and locally. (The percentage split is the founder's own; its source is an open question.)
- Stage 1 is a cheap, low-risk way to learn sourcing and selling, with a profit target (£80-£200 a set) that is concrete.
- Founder keeps the job, so the market test is not a bet-the-house move.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Stages 1 and 3 are commodities with no offer.** Flipped alloys are sold on price against every other seller; "refurbished" is not a reason to pick this seller. Prestige hire has no price, no named buyer pain and no differentiator. The Offers lens finds nothing in either stage that makes a buyer feel stupid saying no. A ladder of three stages where two are price-only is a weak plan.
2. **Stage 2 is still "a cheap car from a flipper".** Private buyers of £2k-£5k cars distrust dealers by default, and no proof is offered: no reviews, no track record, no guarantee. Time and effort are also high for the buyer (viewing, haggling, doubt). Without a guarantee (warranty, short return window, pre-sale inspection the buyer can watch) the founder competes on price, and the stated £500-£1,000 profit target is the first thing a bargain-hunting buyer will squeeze.
3. **The staged funding logic and the pace do not hold.** £80-£200 a set, part-time, may not build enough capital to buy even one £2,000-£5,000 car, and each car is a large cash risk for one person. The jump to luxury hire needs far more capital, insurance and licensing, with an undefined buyer and price. The plan assumes each stage funds the next without a number showing it does.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the Stage 2 buyer you would pick first, and what are they afraid of? What is your guarantee, in one sentence?
- Why would a stranger believe a first-time seller with no reviews? What proof will you have after car one, and after car ten?
- What makes a buyer pick you over a dealer, a private seller or an online car marketplace at the same price? Could you charge £500 more and still be the obvious choice?
- Which Stage 1 or Stage 3 feature would you cut if you had to? Why not go straight to Stage 2 with seed money from savings or a small loan?
- What is the Stage 3 pain? Who hires a chauffeured prestige car from you rather than an established operator, what do they pay, and what is your named offer for weddings or proms?
- How many alloy sets must you sell to fund the first car, and how long will that take on your hours?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Write the Stage 2 offer as a stack with a guarantee (what the buyer gets, what happens if the car fails, in plain words) and a name for it. Test it on a first car before committing more cash.
2. Show that Stage 1 funds Stage 2 with real numbers, or drop Stage 1 as a stage and treat it as a learning side-line.
3. Do not start Stage 3 until Stage 2 shows repeat sales and reviews, and Stage 3 has a named buyer, a price and a differentiator.
