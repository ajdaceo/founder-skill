# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Stage scores: Stage 1 (alloys) 2/10. Stage 2 (used cars) 3/10. Stage 3 (prestige hire) 4/10. Overall 3/10.

## What is strong
- Stage 3 chauffeur-first has a real shape: the founder drives, so it is a service with a personal brand, not a fleet bet. A small niche (weddings, proms, events in Medway/Dartford/Bexley) is a market that could be owned locally, if it is picked narrowly.
- Stage 2 has a differentiator on paper: history check, fresh MOT and inspection report on a £2,000-£5,000 car. In a market full of "sold as seen", that is a trust position, though not a 10x moat.
- Low capital at risk while the Tesco job continues. The ladder funds itself, so failure is cheap.
- Distribution is at least thinkable: online marketplaces for stage 1 and 2, local word of mouth for stage 3.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. No monopoly anywhere on the ladder. Stages 1 and 2 are the most crowded markets a one-person business can enter: every private seller, trader and dealer on the same marketplaces competes for the same buyer on price. The Monopoly lens says competition destroys profit; here it is the whole business. Flipping has no secret, no scale, no network effect, no brand yet.
2. Three businesses, not one. Alloys, cheap cars and chauffeur hire share almost no customers, skills, regulation or reputation. Nothing earned in stage 1 makes stage 2 easier except cash, and cash is not an advantage anyone else lacks. Dominating a small market first is impossible when you start a new market every stage, part-time.
3. Distribution and durability are unproven, and the founder is the bottleneck. Nobody has said how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs. Everything depends on one person's spare hours around a job; a copycat in month six is anyone with a van and a marketplace account. Stage 3 also hinges on licensing and insurance (see questions) that could change the whole model.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. What is the secret: what do you know about sourcing, pricing or prepping that other flippers do not? Name one concrete example.
2. Who else sells the same cars and wheels within 15 miles of the same buyer, and how do you beat them other than on price? (Answered by /founder-competitors.)
3. Which small market do you own first: a car type, a buyer group, a postcode? Why would you be the obvious choice there?
4. How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each cost in time and money? (/founder-consumer for demand and channels; /founder-cfo for cost per sale.)
5. What is your starting capital? Not stated; it decides how fast the ladder can climb. (/founder-cfo.)
6. How many hours a week can you give this around Tesco, and what is the real profit per hour after fees, parts, MOT and fuel, versus £14.70? (/founder-cfo.)
7. Does regular flipping make you a motor trader, and what does that mean for insurance, consumer law and your sales terms? Does chauffeur hire need private hire licensing and hire-and-reward insurance, and at what cost? Get these answered before spending anything on stage 3.
8. What are your stage 3 prices and the demand for prestige chauffeur hire in north Kent? (Unknown; /founder-competitors for rival rates, /founder-consumer for demand.)
9. Why does the ladder need three stages? Why not do only one and get very good at it?
10. Why relocate north of London if it works? You would be a stranger in a bigger, more crowded market.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Pick one stage and one narrow niche (for example, a specific car segment for a specific buyer in one area) and drop the other two until it proves out. Stage 2 with the inspection-report trust offer is the best candidate; treat stage 1 as a skill-building side activity, not a business.
- Produce evidence of an edge: a repeatable sourcing method with documented buy-price versus sell-price on at least a handful of real cars or sets, net of all costs. Numbers must come from /founder-cfo, not estimates.
- Settle the legal position (motor trader status, insurance, consumer law, and for stage 3 private hire licensing and hire-and-reward cover) before scaling or taking on stage 3.
- Show a named, repeatable way to reach buyers with a known cost per sale before moving up the ladder.
- Drop or delay the relocation idea; it is not a plan until a home market is won.
