**Vote: 3 × FUND IF, 0 pass, 0 outright fund. Average score 3.7/10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 4).

Nobody passed, but nobody liked it either. "Fund if" here means "not as pitched".

## Risks more than one member raised (go first)
1. **Three unrelated businesses, no focus.** All three. Different customers, skills, regulation and reputation; only cash carries over, and cash is not an edge. One person part-time cannot dominate any of them.
2. **Stages 1 and 2 are commodity price fights.** All three. No secret, no scale, no brand, no reviews. Stage 2's history check + MOT + inspection report is the only real differentiator, and it is not yet backed by a guarantee or proof.
3. **Stage 3 is a wish, not a product.** All three. No price, no named buyer pain, no differentiator versus established operators, and unresolved licensing/insurance.
4. **The ladder funding is unproven.** Offers and Monopoly. £80-£200 a set may never fund one £2,000-£5,000 car; Stage 3 needs far more.
5. **Stage 2 hidden quality and aftersales.** Offers and Product. Who inspects and preps? What is promised when a fault appears in week two?
6. **Legal and insurance unknowns.** Monopoly and Product. Motor trader status, trade insurance, consumer law; private hire licensing and hire-and-reward cover.
7. **Relocation north of London is premature.** Monopoly and Product.

## Where they disagree
- **Stage 3.** Monopoly rates it the best stage (4: a chauffeur-led local niche could be owned). Product rates it the worst (2: no experience defined). Offers sits between (3).
- **Stage 1.** Product treats it as a time-limited funding step; Monopoly calls it a skill-building side activity at most; Offers says prove it funds Stage 2 with numbers or drop it.

## Conditions checklist
- [ ] Cut to Stage 2 as the one product, in one sentence for one named customer.
- [ ] Stage 2 offer written as a named stack with a plain-words guarantee; test on a first car.
- [ ] Define the full Stage 2 buyer journey (listing, viewing, report, handover, paperwork, aftersales).
- [ ] Name who inspects and preps each car, to what standard; produce a sample inspection report.
- [ ] Write the week-two fault policy before the first sale.
- [ ] Show with real numbers that Stage 1 funds Stage 2, or drop it (time-limited, with an end date) *(CFO)*.
- [ ] Evidence of an edge: documented buy vs sell on a handful of real cars/sets, net of all costs *(CFO)*.
- [ ] A named, repeatable way to reach buyers with a known cost per sale *(consumer, CFO)*.
- [ ] Settle motor-trader status, trade insurance and consumer-law position before selling.
- [ ] Stage 3 parked until Stage 2 has repeat sales and reviews; then needs a named buyer, price, differentiator, and licensing/insurance settled *(competitors, consumer, CFO)*.
- [ ] Drop or delay relocation until a home market is won.

## Strongest version the board can see
Not the three-stage ladder. A single, narrow **Stage 2 business**: "a documented, inspected £2,000-£5,000 car for nervous first-time and family buyers in Medway/Dartford/Bexley", with a written guarantee and a sample report, started small with your own savings while you keep Tesco. Alloys are at most a short, time-boxed way to learn sourcing and raise a little cash; chauffeur hire is parked until Stage 2 has sales and reviews. This differs from what you pitched, which was a sequence of three businesses; the board thinks the sequence is the main weakness.
