# CFO note: Stage 1 alloy wheels (GBP; the tool prints "$" but every number here is £)

Inputs: the desktop assistant's eBay UK active-listing data (13 Oct 2026, asking prices only, no sold data), Checkatrade/forum refurb figures, and the pitch prices tested on the panel. Scenario inputs marked ASSUMPTION or ESTIMATE are not researched. Files: `founder/cfo/*.json`.

## Per-set result (contribution = sale price minus stock minus DIY supplies)

| Scenario | Stock cost / set | Supplies | Sale | Profit per set |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

Supplies £80 = the one forum figure (about £20 a wheel for primer, colour and lacquer). A 5-can kit listing at £35.79 suggests it could be about half that. Not verified.

## The finding that matters
- Salvage-yard singles (£35-£63 each, so £140-£252 a set) **do not work** for this plan. They cost as much as the finished set sells for.
- Unrefurbished 5x112 17 inch sets are already listed at about £157-£260 asking. A refurbished set at £280 sits at the top of that range, so you cannot rely on a big premium.
- **Maximum you can pay for a set** to make £100 profit, with £80 supplies: £80 if you sell at £260, £100 if £280. With £36 supplies: £124 / £144. That means buying whole sets at roughly £25-£35 a wheel or less, or free.
- The £80-£200 profit target from the start only happens on free or very cheap stock. On the evidence so far it is the exception, not the rule.

## What £1,000 a month needs
At £60 profit a set (the £120-stock, £260-sale case) you need about 17 sets a month, roughly 4 a week. At £80 a set, about 13. The model's 8 sets a month at £60 is £480 a month, not £1,000. Year 1 profit of about £4,500 in the £260 case is about £375 a month on average.

## The line to watch
Buying price. Moving stock cost by £40 a set changes profit per set by as much as moving the sale price from £240 to £280. Ahead of price, find a supply of whole sets at £120 or less.

## Cash
Startup is only the kit, put at £100 (ESTIMATE). Stock is the real cash use: 2 sets in stock at £120 is about £240. Your £800 a month covers this easily.

## Not in these numbers
Fees (eBay final value fees, if you sell there; Marketplace local is free), your hours, returns under the 7-day policy, tyres, delivery and fuel. Days-to-sell is not published; measure it on your own first sets. Check the figures with an accountant before relying on them, including how trading income is taxed.

## Decision
- Price: list at £280, accept £240 (unchanged from the panel read), and only buy sets that leave at least £80-£100 profit at £260.
- Do not buy salvage singles.
- Stage 1 as a £1,000 a month business is not supported by the evidence yet. Treat it as a capital builder worth maybe £300-£500 a month at first, while you test whether cheap whole sets exist.
- Re-run the CFO with real purchase prices after your first 3-5 sets.
