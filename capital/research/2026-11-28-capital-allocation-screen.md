# Capital allocation screen (2026-11-28)

Brief: MANDATE-AMENDMENT-2.md. CAPITAL is to act as an operator and capital allocator: find where agents, software, information and capital earn a return, establish the facts for promising ones, and become self-financing.

## Conclusion
**The evidence does not yet justify asking the owner for investment beyond small, capped validation tests.** Twelve mechanisms have now been examined in three rounds (eight in `2026-11-28-mechanism-screen.md`, four here). None shows credible evidence of a strong return for a small operator. Where capital can be deployed directly (trading, buying assets, buying and reselling goods), the realistic return at this scale is small in pounds or negative. Where a business can be built, customer acquisition remains the constraint, and capital spent on acquisition without a proven conversion rate has an expected return near zero.

So the next pound should buy evidence, not scale: the cheapest test that produces a real conversion number.

## The four capital-using mechanisms examined
Sources: `research/mechanisms/acquire.txt`, `arbitrage.txt`, `outreach.txt`, `trading.txt` (mostly secondary sources; estimates are labelled) and `trading/README.md` (first-hand).

### Buying small income-producing online assets (£200 to £5,000): REJECTED at this size
- Assets this small earn too little: listings under $5,000 on one marketplace show a few hundred dollars of revenue a year (for example $2,500 asked for $638 of annual revenue). Running costs can exceed the profit.
- Verification is thin where it is needed most: one large marketplace says vetting "only really kicks in" at $25,000.
- The main cheap category, content and affiliate sites, is declining: sales down 39% in the first half of 2026; AI summaries cut search clicks by about 40 to 58%.
- Agent's estimate: 0 to 15% a year expected at £5,000 with a 30 to 40% chance of a net loss; negative at £1,000.
- Revisit when CAPITAL has retained profits of £10,000 or more and can demand payment-processor access to verify twelve months of revenue.

### Buying and reselling goods on price gaps: REJECTED for now
- No independent data on net returns for any variant. The best case, retail arbitrage into Amazon through a preparation centre, is estimated at 8 to 20% over three months on £2,000, with a real risk of account suspension and withheld funds, and it is not hands-off.
- Domains: 1 to 4% sell each year; renewals consume the gains. Tickets: not viable (planned resale price cap). Vehicles: £5,000 or more per car and heavy hands-on work.

### Finding firms with a measurable weakness and selling the fix: POSSIBLE, weak
- Evidence is thin: generic cold email gets 1 to 4% replies on vendor benchmarks; the agent's estimate is 1 to 4 clients per 1,000 firms contacted.
- Lawful by email only to UK limited companies and to US businesses (with an address and opt-out). Sole traders, Germany, Canada and Australia need consent. AI voice calls need consent in the US.
- The main mailbox provider's policy prohibits unsolicited commercial email in volume. Tools for this exact pitch already exist, so there is no edge.

### Rule-based trading: NOT a source of capital at this scale; a possible treasury policy later
First-hand results (details in `trading/README.md`):
- A published trend rule on five asset classes returned 5.0% a year after costs over 2007 to 2026 with a worst fall of 12%, against 6.0% and a 43% fall for holding the same assets. It reduces risk; it does not add return.
- A trend rule on Bitcoin and Ether returned 9 to 23% a year out of sample (2022 to 2026) with falls of 39 to 56%, and one small change of setting erases the Ether gain. Under five years of data cannot separate that from luck.
- Published evidence on active retail trading is dominated by losses (for example 74 to 89% of retail leveraged accounts in the EU study; 97% of persistent day traders in the Brazilian study).
- Paper trading started 2026-11-28 (`trading/paper/ledger.csv`). No real money.

## Proposed allocation policy
1. **Validation tier.** Capped tests of up to about £100 each, only where loss is limited to the stake and a real conversion number arrives within 30 days. One at a time unless they share set-up.
2. **Scale tier.** £250 to £1,000 into a channel only after a test shows that the cost of winning a customer is recovered within three months on real sales.
3. **Treasury tier.** Retained profit beyond three months of operating costs sits in savings or, if separately approved, a low-cost diversified rule such as rule A. No leverage. Speculative rules stay on paper.
4. **Asset tier.** Purchases only from retained profit, only with twelve months of revenue verified through the payment processor, and only below about two years of verified profit.
5. **Review.** Each operation is judged on return on the capital it has actually used; retained profit goes to whichever has the strongest demonstrated return.
