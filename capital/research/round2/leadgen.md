
# Lead generation thread: 3 candidates (27 Nov 2026)

Method: 27 web searches, snippets only (page fetching blocked). Most price evidence comes from vendor or competitor pages, so treat figures as indicative. I found almost no audited evidence of small independent operators' profits; where that is missing I say so.

## Headline finding

**Raw trigger-event feeds are already commoditised.** Every public-record feed I checked has many near-identical sellers at £9-£90/month. This is the "cloned within weeks" pattern you already identified.

One listed public-data lead product shows $34 revenue in 30 days (EVIDENCE: https://trustmrr.com/startup/leadprysm).

**Conclusion (ASSUMPTION, reasoned from the above):** do not sell feeds. The money sits one step downstream, in outcomes: conversations, funded deals, exclusive enquiries. Buyers pay 10-100x more there, and reputation and response data compound.

## Shared legal ground (all three)

- **Email to companies is permitted.** EVIDENCE: the PECR consent rule for email does not apply to corporate subscribers, and generic addresses such as info@ count as corporate (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/).
- **Sole traders and partnerships need consent.** ASSUMPTION (my understanding of ICO guidance, not confirmed by search): they are treated as individual subscribers, so we email limited companies, LLPs and public bodies only.
- **UK GDPR still applies to named people.** ASSUMPTION: we need a legitimate-interests assessment, a privacy notice, an opt-out in every message, a suppression list and ICO registration (small annual fee, amount unverified).
- **Enforcement is live.** EVIDENCE: the ICO fined two firms £225k in total in January 2026 for unlawful marketing messages (https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/01/fines-of-225-000-for-nuisance-marketing-messages).
- **Planning register terms.** ASSUMPTION, unverified: many council planning portals restrict reuse of applicants' personal data for marketing. Check per council and use company applicants and agents only.
- **Personal injury is off limits.** EVIDENCE: referral fees are banned in personal injury claims (https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/ban-personal-injury-referral-fees). Avoid personal injury, claims and consumer credit entirely.

## Candidate 1: Signal-to-conversation desk for commercial construction specialists

We sell introductions, not data: priced per qualified conversation, with an exclusive trade-by-region territory.

1. **Who pays.** ASSUMPTION: UK specialist subcontractors and suppliers with 5-50 staff and no business-development person. Examples: fire-stopping, M&E, fit-out, scaffolding, groundworks, surveyors, planning consultants.
2. **Problem.** They win work by reaching the developer, architect or main contractor before packages are let.
   - EVIDENCE: the big databases are quote-only on 12-month auto-renewing contracts with 90 days' notice (https://sitelens.co.uk/insights/glenigan-vs-barbour-abi/).
   - ASSUMPTION: the £39 feeds deliver rows of data nobody at the firm has time to work.
3. **Current spend.**
   - EVIDENCE: Glenigan and Barbour ABI are reported at roughly £3,500-£10,000/yr; these are competitor-sourced estimates (https://sitelens.co.uk/compare/glenigan/, https://www.evandeli.com/blog/commercial-construction-leads-without-a-huge-contract).
   - EVIDENCE: commercial or specialised construction leads run £150-£500+ each (https://www.sitesamurai.co.uk/blog/construction-lead-generation-1786586427772).
   - EVIDENCE: UK appointment setting costs £100-£500 per meeting, or £2,000-£8,000/mo on retainer (https://jumplead.io/blog/b2b-appointment-setting-uk, https://orrjo.com/b2b-lead-generation-pricing, https://www.neongorilla.co.uk/blog/appointment-setting-retainer-vs-pay-per-meeting).
   - EVIDENCE: Constructionline membership is about £489/yr (https://planyard.com/blog/top-10-construction-lead-generation-platforms).
4. **Frequency.** ASSUMPTION: continuous. Planning decisions and contract awards publish daily, and each client needs new pipeline every month.
5. **Reaching customers and sourcing leads.**
   - Customers: email limited-company specialists with a free sample of five live projects matched to their trade and area. The sample is the pitch.
   - Leads: council planning portals, Find a Tender and Contracts Finder award notices, and Companies House. Outreach on the client's behalf goes to company applicants, agents and architects, clearly identified, with opt-out.
6. **Price.** ASSUMPTION: £300-£450/mo per exclusive trade-by-region, or £120-£200 per qualified reply or meeting. This sits below agency retainers and above feeds.
7. **Automation.**
   - Automated: scraping, classification, matching, drafting and sending, reply triage, reporting, billing.
   - Not automated: phone calls, which many construction buyers prefer; contract signature; dispute handling.
8. **Hard to replicate.** ASSUMPTION:
   - Exclusive territories lock competitors out one trade and region at a time.
   - An accumulated dataset of which signal, role and message led to a reply and then a win.
   - A history of developers, agents and main contractors, plus the suppression list.
   - Sender reputation and client case studies.
   - A feed clone has none of these.
9. **Willingness to pay.** EVIDENCE: the price points in point 3, and at least seven paying-subscriber planning lead services (headline table). I found no evidence that a small operator profits from this exact outcome-priced model.
10. **Validation (about £20-£30 for a domain and mailbox, two weeks).**
    - Step A: email 150 limited-company specialists in one trade and two regions with free samples.
    - Step B: for the first pilot client, contact 100 applicants or agents.
    - Pass: at least 3 firms accept a paid pilot (at least 1 pays), and at least 5% positive replies in step B.
    - Fail: fewer than 2 pilot acceptances, or under 2% positive replies.
11. **Path (ASSUMPTION).**

    | Monthly profit | Clients needed |
    |---|---|
    | £100 | 1 pilot client |
    | £1,000 | 4 at £300 |
    | £5,000 | about 14 at £400 |
    | £10,000 | about 25-28 at £400-£450, across roughly 6 trades and 5 regions |

    Costs are mailboxes and compute, under 10% of revenue. Allow 9-18 months.
12. **Human involvement.** ASSUMPTION: 1-2 hours a week for contracts, the occasional client call and complaint handling.

- **Legal risks.** Sole-trader recipients; reuse of planning-register personal data; sending in clients' names, where we would act as processor or joint controller and need a contract.
- **Strongest failure reason.** AI-written cold email is saturating, so reply rates may be too low for outcome pricing. Construction is also phone-led and relationship-led.
- **Underserved segment.** Specialist subcontractors too small for Glenigan and too busy to work a £39 feed.

## Candidate 2: Trigger-led introducer for development and commercial finance

We find limited companies with a visible, dated need for finance and introduce them to brokers for a share of the fee.

1. **Who pays.** Commercial, bridging and development finance brokers, on completion.
2. **Problem.** ASSUMPTION: borrower acquisition is their main constraint; they run public introducer schemes to get it.
3. **Current spend.**
   - EVIDENCE: brokers offer introducers up to 50% of client and lender fees (https://cfbuk.org/introducers/) or up to 25% (https://westonfinancialltd.co.uk/introducers/). These are advertised maxima.
   - EVIDENCE: one lender advertises up to 8% commission on drawdown (https://www.nationwidefinance.co.uk/introducers.html).
   - EVIDENCE: lender-paid broker commission is typically 1-2% of the facility (https://marketinvoice.co.uk/commercial-finance-broker/).
   - EVIDENCE: bridging broker fees are 0.5-1.5% of the loan, or £995-£2,995 fixed (https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/finance-guide/bridging-loan-broker-fees/).
   - I found no per-lead price for business loan leads.
4. **Frequency.** ASSUMPTION: every planning approval for a 3-30 unit scheme, auction purchase, new charge at Companies House or public contract award is a possible funding event. These occur daily nationwide.
5. **Reaching customers and sourcing leads.**
   - Brokers: apply to their published introducer programmes; no advertising needed.
   - Borrowers: planning approvals where the applicant is a limited company, the Companies House charges register, and contract award notices. Send a factual email offering a broker introduction, and pass details on only with explicit agreement.
6. **Price.** ASSUMPTION: 20-30% of the broker's earnings. On a £500k facility with a £5k-£10k broker fee, that is about £1,000-£2,500 per completion.
7. **Automation.**
   - Automated: signal mining, qualification, outreach, handover, pipeline chasing.
   - Not automated: signing introducer agreements, any identity or due-diligence steps brokers require, and the broker's own sales conversation.
8. **Hard to replicate.** ASSUMPTION: a dataset linking triggers to funded outcomes, broker relationships and negotiated splits, and a growing base of repeat-borrower developers who come back for each scheme.
9. **Willingness to pay.** EVIDENCE: several brokers publicly advertise introducer commission (URLs in point 3). I found no evidence of what introducers actually earn.
10. **Validation (about £20, two weeks).**
    - Sign two introducer agreements, which are free.
    - Email 200 limited-company applicants with recent small-scheme approvals.
    - Pass: at least 8 request an introduction and brokers accept at least 3 as qualified.
    - Fail: fewer than 3 requests, or brokers decline cold-sourced introductions.
    - Limitation: a funded deal cannot be proven in two weeks.
11. **Path (ASSUMPTION: £1,500 average payout, 5-10% of introductions complete, 3-6 month lag).**

    | Monthly profit | Completions | Accepted introductions per month |
    |---|---|---|
    | £100-£1,000 | 1 every 1-2 months | about 10 |
    | £5,000 | 3-4 a month | 40-60 |
    | £10,000 | 6-7 a month | 80-120 |

    Income is lumpy; expect none for the first 3-4 months.
12. **Human involvement.** ASSUMPTION: the owner signs agreements, appears as the named introducer and reconciles commission statements, about 1-2 hours a week. Brokers may want a human contact.

- **Legal risks.**
  - EVIDENCE: introducing individuals to lenders is regulated credit broking (https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/2/8.html, https://memaconsultants.com/tools/perimeter-assessment/credit-lead-generator).
  - EVIDENCE: limited-company-only arrangements are often outside the regime (https://www.nonregulated.co.uk/sectors). The exact exclusion was not confirmed; take legal advice before launch, and exclude sole traders, small partnerships and anything secured on a home.
  - ASSUMPTION: commission disclosure to the borrower is expected.
- **Strongest failure reason.** Established developers already have brokers, so those who answer cold email skew unfundable. Payout depends on the broker's honesty and takes months.
- **Underserved segment.** First-time and small developers, and SMEs that have just won contracts and need working capital.

## Candidate 3: Exclusive-enquiry sites for high-value commercial trades

Niche quote-request sites that own search demand and sell or rent enquiries exclusively.

1. **Who pays.** ASSUMPTION: UK commercial and specialist trade firms where one job is worth thousands.
2. **Problem.** Shared, low-quality marketplace leads.
   - EVIDENCE: MyBuilder shortlists are typically shared among 3-5 trades (https://localadder.co.uk/blog/mybuilder-review/).
   - EVIDENCE: a competitor study claims 28% of paid Bark leads are disputed; the methodology is unclear (https://www.tradematch.uk/data/lead-platform-pricing-uk-2026).
   - EVIDENCE: comparison sites report Rated People closed to tradespeople in September 2026 and folded into Checkatrade (https://localsearchnorth.uk/blog/checkatrade-vs-bark-vs-mybuilder/).
3. **Current spend.**
   - EVIDENCE: Bark about £5-£36 per lead and MyBuilder £5-£35 (https://localadder.co.uk/uk-trade-lead-cost-tracker/).
   - EVIDENCE: UK median for B2B services is £30 per lead; cold rooms £23, shop signs £15 (https://worklanded.com/lead-price-index).
   - EVIDENCE: B2B pay-per-lead is £30-£150 (https://optimodomarketing.co.uk/b2b-lead-generation-cost-uk/).
   - EVIDENCE: US rank-and-rent sites rent for $500-$2,000/mo; course-seller sources (https://il.ly/blog/rank-and-rent, https://penluma.com/blog/make-money-with-ai/17-local-lead-generation/).
4. **Frequency.** ASSUMPTION: continuous buyer search demand, with monthly spend per tradesperson.
5. **Reaching customers and sourcing leads.**
   - Leads: organic search only, through content that is actually useful, such as cost data, and no fake Google Business listings.
   - Buyers: once enquiries flow, forward the first few free to one limited-company firm per area, then offer exclusivity.
6. **Price.** ASSUMPTION: £25-£80 per exclusive commercial enquiry, or £200-£500/mo per site and area.
7. **Automation.**
   - Automated: site building, content, enquiry qualification, routing, billing.
   - Not automated: Google ranking outcomes, phone-call qualification, buyer disputes.
8. **Hard to replicate.** ASSUMPTION: aged ranking sites, enquiry history and accumulated real price data. This is the weakest moat of the three.
9. **Willingness to pay.** EVIDENCE: marketplace prices in point 3. The only operator data found is one publisher's 20-day report: 124 calls, 15 converted leads, $1,066.90 revenue (https://leadsmartinc.com/news/case-study-rank-and-rent-20-day-report).
10. **Validation (under £30, two weeks).** Ranking cannot be tested in two weeks, so test the buyer side.
    - Email 100 limited-company firms in two niches offering exclusive enquiries at a stated price.
    - Pass: at least 5 written commitments to buy.
    - Fail: fewer than 2.
    - In parallel, launch two sites and measure indexing and impressions only.
11. **Path (ASSUMPTION).**

    | Monthly profit | What it takes |
    |---|---|
    | £100 | 1 rented site, month 4-6 |
    | £1,000 | 4 sites |
    | £5,000 | about 15 sites |
    | £10,000 | 25-30 sites or about 250 enquiries at £40; 18-30 months |

    Many sites will never rank.
12. **Human involvement.** ASSUMPTION: about 1 hour a week for domains, payments and disputes.

- **Legal risks.** Consent wording for passing on consumer enquiries, misleading-impression rules for sites that look like a trader, and call recording.
- **Strongest failure reason.** Search dependence.
  - EVIDENCE: top-position click-through is about 58% lower when an AI Overview shows, though commercial queries are less affected (https://syncgtm.com/blog/how-does-ai-affect-seo-traffic-and-lead-gen, https://rexultz.com/blog/ai-overviews-affiliate-sites-2026-data/).
  - EVIDENCE: the model is less profitable than in 2020 because of the map pack and spam policies (https://www.leadscrape.com/local-lead-generation.html).
  - EVIDENCE: first income takes 3-6 months (https://penluma.com/blog/make-money-with-ai/17-local-lead-generation/).
- **Underserved segment.** Commercial specialists who are badly matched by consumer marketplaces, plus trades displaced from Rated People.

## Considered and not advanced

- **Affiliate programmes.** Payouts are real: HubSpot pays 30% recurring for 12 months, and one fintech network pays up to $500 per referral (EVIDENCE: https://supademo.com/blog/saas-affiliate-programs, https://getreditus.com/blog/best-saas-affiliate-programs). But the model is search-dependent, with publishers reporting 20-40% affiliate revenue drops (EVIDENCE: https://www.revshare.so/reports/affiliate-ai-2026).
- **Intent data for small sellers.** Apollo at $49 per seat and free tiers cap what a small seller will pay for a tool (EVIDENCE: https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/intent-data-pricing/). The gap is done-for-you service, which Candidate 1 covers.
- **US pay-per-call.** Prices are high: roofing $75-$300 per lead, HVAC $30-$250 (EVIDENCE: https://resultcalls.com/blog/how-much-do-home-service-leads-cost-in-2026-prices-by-trade). It needs ad spend or search rankings and falls under US telemarketing rules.

## Honest ranking

Candidate 1 is strongest: it is the only one where the buyer, the price points (£100-£500 per meeting, £150-£500+ per commercial lead, £3.5k-£10k/yr incumbents) and a clear gap between £39 feeds and five-figure agencies are all evidenced, it can be tested for under £30 in two weeks, and revenue starts in month one. Its real risk is cold-email reply rates and the lack of a phone channel. Candidate 2 has the highest value per success and needs no selling to the payer, but it has a 3-6 month cash lag, depends on brokers' honesty, needs a legal check on the FCA perimeter, and I found no evidence of actual introducer earnings; run it as a low-cost side test using the same planning data as Candidate 1. Candidate 3 is weakest: slow, Google-dependent, under AI-answer pressure, and supported mainly by course-seller claims plus one small real dataset; pursue it only if Candidate 1's buyer outreach shows firms asking for inbound enquiries. Across all three, the evidence for buyer prices is decent, the evidence that a small independent operator reaches £10k/month profit is thin, and every customer count and conversion rate above is an assumption to be tested.
