# Round 2: business-model discovery, 2026-11-27

Method change (owner directive): stop looking for small utilities and marketplace apps. Look for businesses with compounding advantages that an AI can operate. Six threads; full reports with sources in `research/round2/`. Limits unchanged: search summaries only, about 25 searches per thread, no page access. Customer counts, conversion rates and paths to £10k are ASSUMPTIONS unless a source is cited in the thread report.

## What the evidence says about business shape
- Base rate for quick software is poor and worsening: median payment-verified indie software revenue about $145/month; the 2026 cohort's median is $80 (TrustMRR-derived, vendor analysis).
- No documented AI-operated business is profitable without a human's audience or a subsidy. Autonomous shop experiments lost money, mainly through customers talking the agent into discounts and giveaways. Rule adopted: fixed prices, rule-based fulfilment, no negotiation by the agent.
- Raw public-record feeds are commoditised: UK planning leads (8+ sellers at £9 to £149/month), tenders, new companies, licences, food openings. One such product shows $34 revenue in 30 days.
- Content/SEO sites, domain flipping, marketplace scrapers and AI-training licensing for small holders are all negative on 2026 evidence.
- Search still works for buying-intent queries; AI summaries hit informational queries about seven times as often.
- UK law allows unsolicited email to limited companies and LLPs, not sole traders or partnerships. Small, researched sends get about 2.5 times the reply rate of bulk.
- The owner is legally responsible for everything the agent does (CMA guidance, March 2026). Buyers expect a named human route.
- Shapes the evidence supports: (1) compounding B2B intelligence subscription, (2) contingency or outcome-priced recovery, (3) human-fronted productised back-office service.
- Largest unknown, in every thread: whether a zero-budget channel can win the first ten B2B customers without a human face. This cannot be settled by research. It needs a test.

## Shortlist (ranked)

### 1. Public-sector supplier and contract-renewal intelligence, one software category at a time
- Who pays: small and mid-sized software vendors selling one category to NHS bodies and councils.
- Problem: they need to know which body uses which supplier, at what cost, ending when. Published notices are incomplete; the answers sit in scattered FOI responses.
- Spend now: Tussell cited at £11,400 per licence per year; Tracker about £5,100; Stotles £50 to £475/month with a free tier.
- Frequency: continuous; 9,416 published IT contracts end in the 12 months to September 2027.
- Reach: free league table per category; email to vendors that are limited companies.
- Price: £149 to £299/month per category, or £400 to £900 per snapshot (assumption).
- Automation: collection, parsing, name matching, drafting and tracking FOI requests. Not automated: owner's identity on requests, judgement on refusals.
- Hard to copy: each FOI round takes 20 working days per body, so history and coverage cannot be scraped in a weekend.
- Willingness to pay: incumbent prices. No evidence yet at our price point.
- Human involvement: 1 to 2 hours a week.
- Why it might fail: Stotles' cheap tiers may be enough; each category has only 20 to 100 possible buyers; reputational cost of commercial FOI use; some bodies restrict commercial re-use.

### 2. Statutory late-payment compensation recovery (contingency)
- Who pays: UK small suppliers routinely paid late by larger companies; fee is a share of money recovered.
- Problem: they are owed £40, £70 or £100 per late invoice plus interest at 11.75%, claimable for six years, and never claim. A credit-management body names the barrier as the effort of analysing six years of sales ledger, which is the work an agent does cheaply.
- Spend now: StatClaim charges 10% no-win-no-fee; general commercial collection 8 to 25%.
- Reach: public pages per large payer built from the government's payment-practices data; free ledger calculator; bookkeeper partnerships.
- Hard to copy: a growing record of which debtors pay statutory claims and after how many letters.
- Second-round check: the Commercial Payments Bill passed the Lords on 20 October 2026 and is in the Commons; most measures expected late 2027 or early 2028. No first-hand evidence found either way on whether large debtors pay these claims without court action. That is the decisive unknown.
- Constraints: we must not conduct litigation (reserved legal activity); templates need a one-off solicitor review (a cost).
- Why it might fail: debtors ignore letters with no litigation behind them, and clients will not risk relationships.

### 3. Customs duty overpayment reclaim for small UK importers (contingency)
- Who pays: importers of record; fee is a share of duty repaid. HMRC is the payer of valid claims, so there is no collection risk.
- Problem: duty overpaid through missed tariff preference and coding errors; three-year reclaim window; source data free from HMRC since 2026.
- Second-round check: more crowded than the thread found. BorderAudit is free to start and £149/month with no success fee; CAT360 has an SME tier; Customs IX and MyCustomsInfo (reports £4.24m recovered) also compete. Mandatory registration of customs intermediaries is proposed (consultation closed 21 September 2026, no outcome found). Complex retrospective claims may take 3 to 6 months.
- Verdict: downgraded to third. Good economics per claim, but the software route is already cheap and a registration regime is coming.

### Held in reserve
- White-label quarterly records desk for small accountancy practices (MTD for Income Tax): strongest dated demand (HMRC estimates 1,077,000 more taxpayers from April 2027) and the buyer already outsources. Needs anti-money-laundering registration and insurance (costs), client-data confidentiality terms, and about 1,800 clients for £10k/month at £20 a quarter. Would reuse the suspended QuarterReady knowledge.
- Outreach desk for construction specialists, paid per qualified conversation: best price evidence (£100 to £500 per meeting) but depends on sending cold email in clients' names, which sits close to the owner's no-spam rule.
- Patent prior-art bounties (about $2,000 per contest): no customer acquisition and a £0 test, but one payer and a ceiling well under £10k/month. Whether AI-prepared entries are allowed is unverified.
- Longitudinal niche price/stock archive: time is the moat, but no revenue for 6 to 12 months and no vertical chosen.

### Rejected this round
Amazon reimbursement, chargebacks, parcel audit, AI bid writing, PDF remediation, grant writing, business rates, freight email automation, wholesale order entry (venture-funded field), domain flipping, content and affiliate sites, scraper marketplaces, AI-training data licensing, arbitrage lead lists (terms-of-service risk), buying micro-SaaS (needs £3k to £5k capital and the most owner time), EU medtech tender pricing (needs human selling), Asian regulatory monitoring (liability, thin moat).

## Validation plan (no product build)
Order: 1, then 2. Each has a pass/fail line; a fail is a stop.

Test 1: contract intelligence. Cost £0 to about £15. Two weeks.
- Pick one category not already profiled by Future Health Intelligence (for example a council back-office system).
- Build a 60-body sample from existing FOI disclosure logs only; send no new FOI requests.
- Publish the sample as a free league table; email 40 vendors in that category (limited companies only) offering the full dataset at £400.
- Pass: 2 paid pre-orders, or 5 replies asking for price or sample. Fail: fewer than 3 replies.
- Needs from owner: network access to public-sector sites; a domain and mailbox (about £15, a cost); approval of the email text before any send; a payment link.

Test 2: late-payment recovery. Cost about £15. Two weeks.
- Landing page with a calculator that takes a CSV of invoice, due and paid dates and shows what is claimable.
- Offer to 30 bookkeeping practices (limited companies) and in small-business communities where promotion is allowed.
- Pass: 5 real ledgers uploaded, 2 clients authorise a claim against at least one former customer, median claimable £1,000 or more. Fail: uploads but nobody authorises.
- Needs from owner: same domain and mailbox; decision on a solicitor review of templates before any claim letter goes out (cost to be quoted first).

What I can do before any of that is approved: choose the category for test 1 from search evidence, draft the email and league-table format, and build the calculator logic for test 2 (a test asset, not a product).
