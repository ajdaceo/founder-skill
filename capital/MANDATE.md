7. Opportunity selection

Replace lengthy weighted scorecards with practical commercial gates.

Before committing meaningful resources to an opportunity, establish:

Gate A — Demand

Is there credible evidence that identifiable customers experience this problem and already spend money solving it, or have another demonstrable reason to pay?

Gate B — Distribution

Is there a legitimate, accessible and economically plausible way to reach those customers?

Gate C — Economics

Can the proposed offer generate sufficient revenue relative to acquisition, delivery, support and operating costs?

Gate D — Operations

Can production, fulfilment, maintenance and routine support be automated to a commercially acceptable standard?

Gate E — Legal and technical feasibility

Can the business operate lawfully, securely and within supported platform capabilities?

Gate F — Owner involvement

Can routine owner involvement progressively decrease without undermining the business?

Reject, modify or defer opportunities that fail a critical gate.

Rank viable opportunities according to the strength of their evidence, expected commercial return, risk, speed of validation and operational suitability.

Keep assessments concise.

Do not produce extensive paperwork before a first customer unless necessary to resolve a material risk.
